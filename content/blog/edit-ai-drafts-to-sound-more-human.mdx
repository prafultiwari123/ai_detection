Using AI to draft a first pass of an email, blog post, or report is common and, in most contexts, perfectly legitimate. The problem is that unedited AI drafts tend to read as generic — flat rhythm, safe language, and a noticeable lack of personal voice. Here's how to actually fix that, rather than just running it through a "humanizer" tool that often makes things worse.

## Vary Your Sentence Length on Purpose

Go through the draft and deliberately break up any string of similarly-sized sentences. Combine two short ones into a longer one, or chop a long one into two short, punchy statements. This single change does more to break up "AI rhythm" than almost anything else.

## Cut the Hedge Words

Search for "generally," "often," "can vary," and "it's important to note" — then either delete them or replace them with a direct claim. If you actually believe something, say it plainly instead of softening it.

## Add Specific Details

Replace vague examples with real ones. Instead of "a busy professional might struggle to find time," write about an actual situation — a client you worked with, a personal anecdote, a specific number or outcome. Specificity is one of the fastest ways to make writing feel human, because AI models default to generalized examples.

## Read It Out Loud

If a sentence sounds stiff or overly formal when spoken, it probably needs editing. Human speech patterns — contractions, occasional sentence fragments, natural pauses — rarely show up in raw AI output, and reading aloud is the fastest way to catch where the draft doesn't sound like you.

## Cut the Redundant Summary

AI drafts often restate the introduction as a conclusion almost word-for-word. Cut this unless the piece is genuinely long enough to need a recap — most short-to-medium content doesn't.

## Inject Your Actual Opinion

If the draft presents "both sides" of something you actually have a clear view on, rewrite it to reflect your real stance. This is often the single biggest difference between AI drafts and compelling human writing — AI hedges, people commit.

## Why This Matters Beyond Just "Sounding Human"

Editing thoroughly isn't about tricking a detector — it's about producing genuinely better writing. Text that keeps your real voice, specific details, and actual opinions reads better for your audience regardless of whether any AI tool touched the first draft. That's the real goal, and it happens to also be what separates writing that resonates from writing that gets skimmed and forgotten.
