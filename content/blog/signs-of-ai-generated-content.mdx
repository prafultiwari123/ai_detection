Whether you're vetting freelance writers, reviewing student work, or just curious about something you read online, these are the most reliable signs that a piece of writing was AI-generated.

## 1. Uniform Sentence Length

Human writers naturally vary their rhythm — short sentences for emphasis, longer ones for detail. AI-generated text often settles into a consistent medium-length pattern across an entire piece, which can feel subtly monotonous even when the grammar is flawless.

## 2. Overuse of Transitional Phrases

Watch for repeated use of phrases like "moreover," "furthermore," "in conclusion," and "it's worth noting." These aren't wrong to use, but AI models tend to lean on them far more heavily than natural human writing does.

## 3. Generic, Non-Specific Examples

AI-generated content often illustrates points with vague, general examples ("for instance, a busy professional might...") rather than the specific, sometimes odd details a human writer pulls from real experience.

## 4. Excessive Hedging and Balance

Phrases like "it can vary," "generally speaking," and "there are pros and cons to consider" show up frequently because models are trained to avoid overly confident or one-sided claims — even on topics where a human writer would happily take a stance.

## 5. Perfect but Flat Grammar

Zero typos, zero awkward phrasing, and zero personality quirks can actually be a red flag rather than a good sign. Human writing usually carries small imperfections, regional phrasing, or stylistic habits unique to the writer.

## 6. Repetitive Structural Patterns

Look for repeated formatting habits — starting every section with a rhetorical question, or always following a claim immediately with a three-item list. These patterns emerge because the model has learned they're statistically common ways to organize information.

## 7. Lack of Genuine Opinion or Voice

AI-generated content often summarizes multiple viewpoints without committing to one, especially on debatable topics. Human writers, especially in blogs, essays, or opinion pieces, are more likely to stake out a clear position and defend it.

## None of These Are Definitive Alone

Any single sign here can appear in genuinely human writing too — especially from writers who learned formal English as a second language, or those who naturally write in a structured, methodical style. The signs are most useful in combination, and even then, they're probabilistic indicators rather than proof. That's exactly why dedicated detection tools exist: they can process these signals systematically across an entire document rather than relying on a reader's gut instinct.
